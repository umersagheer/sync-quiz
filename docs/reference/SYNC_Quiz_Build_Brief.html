01

The product in one read

SYNC sells compounded peptides — injectable compounds like BPC-157 and Sermorelin — through a US telehealth model. Cash-pay, no insurance. You can't just buy them; a licensed provider has to prescribe, so every customer must pass through a real clinical assessment.

That clinical assessment is boring and long. So SYNC puts a quiz in front of it. The quiz asks what you're struggling with, gives you a personalised "here's what fits you" moment, captures your email, and then hands you into the clinical intake with all your answers already filled in.

We are building the quiz and the reveal. Not the clinical intake — that's on Bask, their telehealth backend partner. Our job ends at a button that passes the customer and their answers across.

The reveal screen is the whole point. Everything before it is trust-building; the reveal is where the customer either converts or leaves. Treat it as the deliverable and the fifteen screens before it as the setup.

02

What we're actually building

The system is small, but it has one hard architectural rule: the mapping from a customer's answers to a named compound must never reach the browser. That single constraint decides where everything sits.

03

The flow you'll be implementing

Seven linear screens, then a fan-out into one of five branches, then everything converges again for the cross-signal questions, an email gate, and the reveal. Only one branch ever fires per customer.

04

He calls it "a positioning discipline, not an edge case." Build it as its own route and its own component tree.

06

Storyblok: how it works, and how not to use it

Support for Next.js is first-class. @storyblok/react is on v6 and ships a /rsc entry point built for the App Router — server components render CMS content directly, and the Visual Editor still works. Practically: storyblokInit() with apiPlugin, fetch via getStoryblokApi(), render with StoryblokServerComponent, and spread storyblokEditable(blok) onto elements so clicking them in the editor iframe selects them.

Two tokens, two modes. The editor previews version: "draft" with the preview token over an HTTPS preview URL; production serves version: "published" with the public token, cached, and a Storyblok webhook hits /api/revalidate to call revalidateTag(). Copy changes go live in seconds with no deploy — which is exactly what the client asked for.

Now the part that matters. There are two ways to use Storyblok, and the obvious one is wrong for this project.

Page-builder mode — every screen is a nestable blok, editors drag, drop and reorder, and we render whatever tree the API returns. This is how most Storyblok tutorials work, and it would let a marketer delete the option whose value is gut_mucosal and silently break the REPAIR branch for every customer in it. The failure is invisible until someone lands on a dead route.

Typed content-source mode — Storyblok holds a fixed set of entries against a locked schema. Our code owns the screen order, the state machine and the answer values. We fetch, validate with Zod, and render our own components. This is what we should build. The client's actual stated need is "iterate copy without a deploy." That need is fully met here. What he does not need — and should not have — is the ability to restructure a clinical routing funnel without a review.

07

"There's no backend, right?"

Not quite. There is no separate service — no Nest app, no Railway box. But there is a server, and it is not optional. Next.js route handlers plus one Postgres database, deployed as part of the same Vercel app. Five reasons it can't be a pure client:

  • The engine can't ship to the browser. If the answer-to-compound map is in the JS bundle, anyone can read it out of devtools. That is the exact 503A promotional-claim exposure their clinical lead names in writing.
  • Server-side conversion tracking is mandated. The copy layer says CAPI from the email event onward, with no health-adjacent parameters going to ad platforms. You cannot do that from a pixel.
  • Save-progress needs a durable row keyed by a signed httpOnly cookie. localStorage alone dies on a cleared browser and can't move devices.
  • The Bask handoff token must be signed server-side. The signing secret cannot live in the client.
  • These answers are health data. Once tied to an email and posted to an EMR they are PHI in all but name. They should not sit in localStorage, appear in a URL, or land in a Sentry breadcrumb.

On where the engine should sit — he asked for our view, so give him one: Node runtime route handler, not edge. It runs once per customer, after email capture, so latency is irrelevant; it needs database access and an outbound call to Bask; and edge would add constraints for no gain. Keep the quiz screens themselves static and cached at the edge — they're just CMS copy — and put the single engine call on Node.

Write the engine as a pure TypeScript module with no framework imports, taking an answers object and returning a shape. Then the 18-path test matrix in the mapping doc becomes its test suite, almost literally. That matrix is the most useful thing they've offered; ask for it early.

Stack calls

You asked whether to reach for Zod, forms, Zustand, React Query, shadcn. Here are the calls with the reasoning, so you can defend them rather than just apply them.

The component kit, in full

Twelve components carry ninety percent of the build. Naming them makes the estimate concrete: ScreenShell (gradient layer + progress header + back/close), OptionCard, OptionGroup (single and multi variants over Radix), RecognitionLine (the red bar), EducationPanel (the grey box), PrimaryButton, TextField, ProgressHeader, and on the reveal: BenefitChip, ProtocolCard, PlanSelector, StickyFooter. Build those properly in week one and the fifteen screens assemble quickly.

The gradients

You're right that they're the risky part of the design. They're soft organic mesh gradients that shift hue per section — cream and amber through the opening, warmer through goals, deeper rose in the branches. Three ways to build them, and only one is correct here:

  • Export from Figma as WebP or AVIF, one per section theme. Pixel-exact, 30–70KB each at 2×, background-size: cover, preload the first. Cross-fade between sections by stacking two layers and transitioning opacity. Do this.
  • Hand-authored CSS — three or four radial-gradient blobs in a fixed layer under a blur(80px) filter. Tiny and animatable, but it will not match Figma, and a full-viewport blur filter is a real cost on a mid-range Android, which is what this audience is holding.
  • Canvas or WebGL. No. Battery and first-paint cost on a mobile-first paid-traffic funnel where every hundred milliseconds is conversion.

One thing to check before you commit: in the Figma frames the text sits directly on the gradient with no scrim. On the lighter cream areas near-black type is fine, but check contrast on the deeper rose screens and hold the line at AA. The audience skews 35-plus, so don't let body text go below 16px anywhere.

Every price in the spec is a literal $X. The plan selector, the running total and the sticky footer — the three elements called the conversion mechanism — are all priced surfaces with no numbers behind them.

Flag 04

The reveal copy doesn't exist yet

By their own document, Amelia still owes four base paragraphs, four benefit chips for every compound, base copy for eight single-only molecules, the supporting-protocol pairings and four upsell one-liners. That's roughly thirteen card variants of unwritten copy on the screen that matters most. "Finished designs and finished spec" isn't quite the position.

AskFrame it as the Storyblok argument, not a complaint: we ship the reveal with the schema live and placeholders in place, Amelia fills it in herself without waiting on us. That's a selling point — use it.
Flag 05

Three naming systems that don't line up

The quiz has five lanes: REPAIR, PERFORM, DEFINE, RESTORE, PT-141. The intake has six internal stacks: RECOVER, PERFORM, SCULPT, YOUTH, MIND, OPTIMIZE. And a third set of patient-facing blend names: APEX, IGNITE, RESTORE, AEON, DELTA. RESTORE means a lane in one and a product in another. There is no MIND lane in the quiz at all, and the intake flags its own OPTIMIZE lane as doing double duty and needing a split before launch.

AskWho owns the mapping table between the quiz lanes and the intake stacks? Without it the token handoff sends values Bask can't route. This is a half-hour conversation now and a nasty integration bug later.
Flag 06

Save-progress can't do what he wants it to do

He wants to recover people who bounce before email capture on Screen 9. But with no email and no phone before Screen 9, there is no channel to bring anyone back. Save-progress gets you same-device return via a cookie. It does not recover a bounce — nothing can, without contact details.

Also worth saying plainly: it's fifteen screens, not eleven, and five of those are education interstitials with no input and no data captured. On cold paid mobile traffic that's a lot of pure drop-off surface.

AskThree real options. One, cookie plus server session for same-device resume — cheap, do it regardless. Two, an optional "save my progress" email ask around Screen 6, skippable, which is the only thing that creates a recovery channel before Screen 9. Three, fold the education screens inline rather than giving each its own step. Recommend one and two.
Flag 07

Eligibility is at the wrong end of the funnel

The quiz deliberately has no consent wall — "eligibility lives in the clinical intake, after they care." But the intake's hard gates are age under 21, an uncovered state, pregnancy, and active cancer. So a customer can complete fifteen screens, hand over an email, see a personalised protocol with a price, click through — and get hard-blocked. Worse than a wasted click: you showed a compounded-drug recommendation to someone you were never permitted to treat.

AskMove two cheap gates into the quiz — state of residence and 21-plus — on one early screen. State can double as personalisation so it doesn't read as friction. Leave pregnancy and cancer in the clinical intake where they belong. One screen of cost, and it removes the funnel's worst outcome.
Flag 08

These answers are health data

Pre-consult marketing quiz or not: once an answer set is tied to an email and posted into an EMR, it behaves like PHI. That has build consequences that are cheap now and expensive to retrofit — database region and whether a BAA is available, no answer payloads in logs or Sentry, retention policy, and never putting a lane or an answer in a URL or a page title where it leaks through the referrer.

AskConfirm who is accountable for the data before the handoff — SYNC or Bask. And keep the session id in the URL opaque: /quiz/r/a8f3…, never /quiz/reveal?lane=PT-141.
Flag 09

Two unknowns that change the repo on day one

"You'll be working alongside our current site, not replacing it" — but what is the current site? If it's already Next.js on Vercel, the quiz is a route segment sharing tokens and the Storyblok space. If it's Webflow or WordPress, it's a separate app on a subdomain with cookie-domain and analytics stitching to sort out. Separately, the Bask contract is undefined — their own checklist lists three unconfirmed dependencies, including the patient-creation endpoint schema.

AskWhat's the current site built on, and same repo or subdomain? For Bask, build behind an adapter interface with a stub from the start so an undefined contract never blocks the front end.

How to play the call

Don't open with the problems. Open by showing you've read all three documents more carefully than most agencies would — the fifteen-screen count, the four shapes and two templates, the fact that the upsell fires on exactly one shape. That earns the right to raise flag 01.

Then be straight about it: we can't responsibly quote the reveal until you tell us whether the customer sees a compound name and a price. That's not a hedge, it's the single biggest line item in the build, and a client who hears it from us before the contract will trust the numbers that follow.

Quote phases 1 through 3 with confidence — they're well-specified and low-risk. Put phase 4 behind the answer to flag 01, and offer the ongoing retainer around Storyblok, copy iteration and A/B testing the reveal, which is where the recurring work genuinely is.
